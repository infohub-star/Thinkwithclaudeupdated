Why do Indian families get health insurance claims rejected or only partly paid?

Health insurance complaints rose 41% in a year, mostly over refused, delayed or partly paid claims. Here's "ClaimShield", a claim-dispute copilot one person could build with Claude in a weekend.

Written by Shantanu Anand, AI Trainer & Consultant · 14 November 2026

Insurance & HealthcareItch Score 80Build time: a weekend

The problem

Who feels it: Policyholders and their families who receive a rejection or a short payout after a hospital stay, often with a one-line reason they cannot interpret. Hospital billing desks and small insurance advisors who help them face the same paperwork every week.

  • Where this came from: IRDAI grievance data reported in Indian financial news (Angel One, Coverfox), coverage of regulator action on claim handling (Outlook Money), and personal-finance explainers on partial payouts and rejection reasons (Money9, PrimeInvestor), echoing frequent complaints in Indian personal-finance forums.
  • Complaints are rising fast. Health insurance complaints rose 41% to 1,37,361 in FY25, from 97,503 in FY24, according to IRDAI data (Angel One).
  • Claims are the core issue. Nearly 7 in 10 complaints in general and health insurance were linked to claim refusal, settlement delays, partial payments and documentation disputes (Angel One).
  • Policyholders usually win when they escalate. Insurance ombudsmen resolved 41,055 grievances in FY26, and nearly 79% of rulings favoured policyholders (Coverfox).
  • The regulator has flagged lapses. IRDAI issued show-cause notices to eight insurers over issues including complex customer information sheets and gaps in claim review committees (Outlook Money).

Who's already working on it

ClaimBuddy helps patients and hospitals manage claims and says it has processed claims for over 35,000 patients worth more than Rs 500 crore with 250+ partner hospitals (Indian Startup News). Legal-help services and brokers also assist with disputes. Few self-serve tools read the policy wording and the rejection letter together and draft the escalation for the family.

The solution idea

"ClaimShield" reads your policy and the insurer's rejection or settlement letter, explains each deduction in plain language, checks it against the policy wording, and drafts the grievance and ombudsman complaint.

Letter decoderUpload the rejection or settlement letter; it lists each deduction and the reason code in plain English or Hindi.
Policy checkMatches every deduction to the clause in your policy wording, such as room-rent caps, waiting periods or non-payable items, and flags those that look unsupported.
Escalation draftsWrites the grievance to the insurer, then the Bima Bharosa complaint and the ombudsman application, with the right attachments listed.
Deadline trackerTracks response windows for each stage and reminds the family when to escalate.
Pre-claim checklistBefore discharge, lists the documents and bill items most likely to cause a dispute.

Weekend build plan

  1. Saturday morning — evidence: Collect ten anonymised rejection or short-payout letters with their policy wordings and list every deduction type.
  2. Saturday midday — parser: Use Claude Code to extract deductions from letters and bills into structured data.
  3. Saturday afternoon — policy check: Write the clause-matching logic as a Claude skill and test it on your ten cases.
  4. Saturday evening — drafts: Add the grievance, Bima Bharosa and ombudsman draft generators.
  5. Sunday morning — tracker: Build the deadline tracker and reminder emails.
  6. Sunday midday — summary: Create the shareable one-page claim summary.
  7. Sunday afternoon — pilot: Run it with three families or one small insurance advisor and compare with their own reading of the case.

Starter prompt for Claude Code

Build a Next.js + TypeScript web app called "ClaimShield" for Indian health insurance policyholders.

Flow: the user uploads their policy wording PDF, the insurer's rejection or settlement letter, and the hospital bill -> extract every deduction with amount and stated reason -> call the Claude API to match each deduction to the relevant policy clause and mark it supported, unclear or unsupported, with the clause quoted -> explain the result in plain English or Hindi -> draft a grievance letter to the insurer, and later a Bima Bharosa complaint and an insurance ombudsman application -> track deadlines for each stage and send reminders.

Include: a case dashboard, a one-page shareable summary, sample anonymised documents for tests, and unit tests for the deduction maths. Never give legal guarantees; show a clear disclaimer. Keep all medical data encrypted and private per account.

Riskiest assumption, and how to test it

Assumption: Families may prefer a human expert to handle the dispute and may not trust software with a large medical bill.

Test first: Offer free reviews of ten real disputed claims through an insurance advisor. If at least four cases show a deduction that looks unsupported and two families file the drafted grievance, the product has a case; if not, sell it to advisors as a back-office tool instead.

This is not legal or insurance advice; say so clearly. Health records are sensitive personal data, so handle them under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act with consent and encryption.
