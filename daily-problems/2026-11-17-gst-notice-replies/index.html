Why do small traders in India panic and miss deadlines when a GST notice arrives?

Thousands of small traders have received GST notices built on UPI data or return mismatches, often with short and uneven reply deadlines. Here's "Notice Saathi", a notice-reply assistant a small team could build with Claude in a weekend.

Written by Shantanu Anand, AI Trainer & Consultant · 17 November 2026

Tax & MSME ComplianceItch Score 74Build time: a weekend

The problem

Who feels it: Shopkeepers, bakeries, small hotels, service providers and other small businesses (MSMEs), many of them unregistered or newly registered. A notice arrives in tax language with a deadline, the owner does not know whether it is a demand or a question, and the local accountant is busy. Missing the window can turn a simple query into a demand order with interest and penalty.

  • Where this came from: News reports on GST notices sent to small traders in Karnataka and Madhya Pradesh, plus public discussion among traders and tax professionals (Business Today, Star of Mysore, Free Press Journal).
  • The volume is real. Nearly 6,000 GST notices were issued to Karnataka traders based on UPI transaction data, according to a Business Today report quoting a state tax official (Business Today).
  • Many notices can be closed with the right reply. The official said the notices are proposals, not final demands, and that traders dealing in exempt goods can reply with proof to get a nil demand (Business Today).
  • Silence is costly. In Mysuru Division, 1,314 traders got notices; those who neither reply nor register face suo motu proceedings, and a demand order with interest and penalty can follow within 15 days (Star of Mysore).
  • Deadlines are inconsistent. In Madhya Pradesh, practitioners reported ASMT-10 scrutiny notices with reply windows of 9 to 23 days, against the 30 days experts cite (Free Press Journal).

Who's already working on it

Chartered accountant firms and online services such as Kanakkupillai and LegalRaasta draft replies for a fee. Practice tools like Suvit help accountants track notices, and early AI tools such as GST Reply AI draft responses. Most are built for accountants; few explain the notice to the owner in plain language and collect the evidence from them.

The solution idea

"Notice Saathi" reads a GST notice photo or PDF, explains in plain English or Hindi what it asks and by when, collects the right proof from the owner, and prepares a reply draft for their accountant to approve.

Notice readerUpload a photo or PDF; it identifies the notice type, the section quoted, the amount proposed and the reply deadline.
Plain-language explainerA short summary in English, Hindi or Kannada: is this a question or a demand, what happens if you ignore it, and what to do next.
Evidence checklistA tailored list of documents to upload, such as sales registers, bank statements or proof that goods are exempt.
Reply draftA structured reply draft with annexures, marked for review by a chartered accountant or tax practitioner before filing.
Deadline alertsWhatsApp and email reminders before the reply window closes, with the accountant copied.

  2. Saturday morning: upload and extraction: Use Claude Code to build the upload screen and a Claude API call that returns notice type, section, amount and deadline as JSON.
  3. Saturday afternoon: explainer: Generate the plain-language summary in three languages and test it on five shop owners.
  4. Saturday evening: evidence checklist: Map each notice type to its document list in a skill file and render a checklist with uploads.
  5. Sunday morning: reply draft: Draft the reply from the notice, the owner's answers and the uploaded proof, with a clear review-required banner.
  6. Sunday afternoon: accountant view: Add a reviewer login where the accountant edits and approves the draft.
  7. Sunday evening: reminders and launch: Add scheduled deadline reminders, deploy, and onboard three accountants with their small-business clients.

Starter prompt for Claude Code

Build a Next.js + TypeScript + Postgres web app called "Notice Saathi" for Indian small businesses that receive GST notices.

Flow: owner uploads a photo or PDF of a GST notice -> Claude API extracts notice type, GSTIN, section cited, tax period, amount proposed and reply deadline as JSON -> show a plain-language explainer in English, Hindi or Kannada -> show an evidence checklist based on notice type (load mappings from /skills/gst-notices.md) -> owner uploads documents and answers short questions -> Claude drafts a reply with annexure list -> accountant reviews, edits and approves.

Requirements: every draft carries a banner saying it must be reviewed by a qualified tax professional; deadlines and notice rules live in a config file; daily cron sends reminders 7, 3 and 1 days before the deadline; store documents encrypted. Write tests for extraction on 5 sample notices.

Riskiest assumption, and how to test it

Assumption: Owners may still hand the notice straight to their accountant, and accountants may see the tool as a threat rather than a time-saver.

Test first: Before building, partner with two chartered accountant practices in Bengaluru. Process their next 10 small-client notices manually with Claude: explainer, checklist and first draft. Measure hours saved per notice and ask if they would pay for it. If both say yes and drafts need only light edits, build.

A GST reply has legal and financial consequences. The product must not give tax advice or file replies on its own; a qualified professional must review every draft. Keep rules in an updatable config, and treat notices and bank statements as sensitive data under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act.
