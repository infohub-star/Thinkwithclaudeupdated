Why do small businesses in India take years to recover money from a bounced cheque?

More than 43 lakh cheque-bounce cases are waiting in Indian courts, and many small suppliers miss the strict notice deadlines before they even start. Here's "ChequeChase", a bounced-cheque recovery assistant a small team could build with Claude in a weekend.

Written by Shantanu Anand, AI Trainer & Consultant · 16 November 2026

Legal & MSME FinanceItch Score 76Build time: a weekend

The problem

Who feels it: Traders, distributors, landlords and small manufacturers who still accept post-dated or security cheques from buyers and tenants. When a cheque bounces, the owner has to act within tight legal timelines, find a lawyer, and then wait through hearings scheduled months apart, while the money stays stuck.

  • Where this came from: Court-pendency data reported by legal and news outlets (SCC Online, The Hawk / IANS), an analysis of why the process fails honest complainants (Bar & Bench), and the many public legal guides and business-forum threads where owners ask what to do after a client's cheque bounces.
  • The backlog is huge. The government told Parliament in December 2024 that 43,05,932 cheque-bounce cases were awaiting disposal (SCC Online).
  • Cases drag for years. National Judicial Data Grid figures tabled in the Lok Sabha showed about 44.7 lakh pending cases, of which more than 25.9 lakh had been unresolved for over two years (The Hawk / IANS).
  • They clog the courts. In Delhi, 6,50,283 pending Section 138 complaints in September 2025 were close to half of all criminal case pendency in the capital's trial courts (SCC Online).
  • The courts are pushing for digital processes. Supreme Court guidelines from September 2025 call for electronic service of summons, live dashboards and QR-code or UPI payment links so accused parties can settle early (SCC Online).

Who's already working on it

Legal marketplaces such as LawRato, Rest The Case and Vidhikarya connect owners with lawyers and sell notice drafting. Online dispute resolution platforms offer mediation. Accounting tools track overdue invoices but do not track the legal clock on a bounced cheque. Nobody gives a small business one place that tracks every cheque, warns before each deadline, drafts the documents and pushes for early settlement.

The solution idea

"ChequeChase" logs every cheque a business receives, catches a bounce the day the bank memo arrives, tracks each legal deadline, drafts the demand notice for a lawyer to review, and sends the payer a settle-now payment link.

Cheque registerSnap a photo of each received cheque; it reads payer, amount, date and bank and keeps a register with due dates for deposit.
Bounce captureUpload the bank return memo; it records the reason and the date and starts the statutory countdown automatically.
Deadline trackerShows the days left to send a demand notice and to file a complaint, with reminders on WhatsApp or email well before each date.
Notice draftsDrafts the demand notice and a case summary with all cheque details filled in, ready for a lawyer to review and sign.
Settle-early linkSends the payer a polite reminder with a UPI payment link, because many disputes settle once the notice arrives.

Starter prompt for Claude Code

Build a Next.js + TypeScript + Postgres web app called "ChequeChase" for Indian small businesses.

Flow: user uploads photos of cheques received -> Claude API extracts payer, bank, cheque number, amount and date as JSON -> cheque register. When a cheque bounces, the user uploads the bank return memo -> extract reason and memo date -> create a case and compute the demand-notice and complaint-filing deadlines from a config file (do not hard-code legal rules in logic). Show a dashboard of open cases with days remaining. Generate a demand notice draft from a template in /templates, filled with case data, watermarked DRAFT - FOR LAWYER REVIEW. Send payer reminders by email with a UPI payment link. Add a daily job that emails warnings 7, 3 and 1 days before each deadline. Store files encrypted, add auth, seed with 5 sample cases, and write tests for the deadline calculation.

Riskiest assumption, and how to test it

Assumption: Small business owners may prefer to call their usual lawyer or chase the payer informally, and may not pay for software for an event that happens only a few times a year.

Test first: Before building, talk to 15 distributors or traders who accept cheques. Ask how many bounces they had last year and whether they ever missed a deadline. Offer five of them a free manual tracking service for their open bounces using a shared sheet and Claude drafts. If at least three use it and would pay, build the product.

Demand notices and complaints have legal consequences. The product must present drafts for review by a qualified lawyer and must not give legal advice. Keep timelines in an updatable config, and handle cheque images as sensitive financial data under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act.
