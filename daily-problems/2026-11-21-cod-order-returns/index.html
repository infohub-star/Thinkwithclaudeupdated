Why do small online sellers in India lose money on cash-on-delivery orders that come back undelivered?

More than a third of cash-on-delivery orders for small online brands come back unopened, and the seller pays shipping both ways. Here's "DoorCheck", a WhatsApp order-confirmation and risk-scoring assistant buildable with Claude in a weekend.

Written by Shantanu Anand, AI Trainer & Consultant · 21 November 2026

E-commerce & D2CItch Score 78Build time: a weekend

The problem

Who feels it: Small direct-to-consumer brands, Instagram and WhatsApp sellers, and small Shopify stores, especially those selling into Tier 2 and Tier 3 towns. They pay forward and return shipping, lose packaging and stock time, and often find out only days later that the buyer refused the parcel, gave a wrong address or never meant to buy.

  • Where this came from: No problem list was readable this run, so this was chosen from logistics industry reports and seller discussions, including ClickPost, Amazon Shipping and a Shopify merchant thread.
  • Cash on delivery is still the default. Roughly 60 to 65% of Indian e-commerce orders are placed as cash on delivery, per ET Prime Research cited by Amazon Shipping (Amazon Shipping).
  • Those orders fail far more often. On ClickPost's FY2026 direct-to-consumer network, 37.51% of cash-on-delivery orders ended as return-to-origin, against 5.28% of prepaid orders (ClickPost).
  • Each failed order costs real money. Amazon Shipping gives an illustrative Rs 180 to 240 logistics cost per returned order; ClickPost estimates about Rs 350 on a Rs 1,200 order once acquisition and stock costs are included (Amazon Shipping, ClickPost).
  • Speed and addresses matter. Shipway FY25 data shows return rates rising from 22% when delivery is attempted within 1 to 2 days to 35% after 5 or more days, and address errors are estimated to cause 18 to 24% of returns (Amazon Shipping).
  • Smaller towns are harder. Blended return rates were 12.0% in Tier 3 towns against 6.6% in metros in October to November 2025 (ClickPost).

Who's already working on it

GoKwik offers return-risk prediction to larger brands and says it saved brands Rs 130 crore in such losses in 2023 (CXOToday). Shiprocket, ClickPost and Shipway bundle return-reduction tools into shipping and tracking software, and Amazon offers cash-on-delivery suppression to its own sellers. These mostly serve brands with checkout integrations and volume; we didn't find a simple, low-cost tool built for the small seller who takes orders over Instagram and WhatsApp.

The solution idea

"DoorCheck" confirms every cash-on-delivery order over WhatsApp, fixes the address, scores the return risk, and nudges risky buyers to prepay before the parcel ships.

WhatsApp confirmationSends each new cash-on-delivery order a short confirmation message with order details; unconfirmed orders are held, not shipped.
Address repairReads the free-text address, checks pincode and landmark, and asks the buyer one clear question when something is missing.
Risk scoreScores each order from past outcomes by pincode, order value, repeat buyer and response behaviour, with a plain-language reason.
Prepay nudgeOffers high-risk buyers a small discount or a partial advance by UPI link to convert to prepaid.
Weekly loss reportShows returns avoided, shipping saved and which pincodes or products cause the most failures.

Weekend build plan

  1. Saturday morning — collect data: Get 3 to 6 months of order exports with delivery outcomes from two or three small sellers you know.
  2. Saturday midday — baseline score: Ask Claude Code to compute return rates by pincode, order value and repeat buyer, and build a simple weighted score.
  3. Saturday afternoon — address parser: Use the Claude API to clean 200 real addresses and measure how many need a follow-up question.
  4. Saturday evening — prototype: Generate an artifact of the dashboard and confirmation flow and show it to two sellers.
  5. Sunday morning — WhatsApp flow: Wire a WhatsApp Business API sandbox for confirmation, address question and UPI prepay link.
  6. Sunday midday — order intake: Connect Shopify webhooks or a Google Sheet so new orders flow in automatically.
  7. Sunday afternoon — report: Add the daily outcome sync and weekly loss report as scheduled jobs.
  8. Sunday evening — pilot: Turn it on for one seller's live orders with manual approval before any message goes out.

Starter prompt for Claude Code

Build a Next.js + TypeScript + Postgres web app called "DoorCheck" for small Indian online sellers.

Flow: new cash-on-delivery order arrives (Shopify webhook or Google Sheet row)
-> Claude API cleans the address into JSON (name, line1, landmark, city, pincode, missing_fields)
-> risk score from historical outcomes (pincode return rate, order value band, repeat buyer, reply time)
-> send WhatsApp confirmation via the WhatsApp Business API sandbox; if high risk, offer a UPI prepay link with a small discount
-> hold unconfirmed orders; release confirmed ones to the shipping queue.

Include: seller dashboard (orders, risk reason, status), CSV import of past orders with outcomes, a nightly job that recomputes pincode return rates, a weekly loss report, seed data, and tests for the address parser and scorer. Keep all messages short, polite and available in English and Hindi.

Monetisation

Suggested pricing, not yet validated.

  • Free: up to 100 orders a month.
  • Rs 1 to 2 per confirmed order, or Rs 999 a month up to 1,500 orders (not validated).
  • Channel: Shopify app listing, seller WhatsApp groups and shipping aggregator partnerships.

Riskiest assumption, and how to test it

Assumption: Buyers may ignore or distrust confirmation messages from small sellers, so confirmation rates could be too low to cut returns meaningfully.

Test first: Before building integrations, run the flow manually for one seller for two weeks: send confirmation and address messages by hand from a template, and compare the return rate of confirmed orders against the seller's last three months. A reasonable bar: at least 60% of buyers reply, and returns on confirmed orders fall by a quarter or more.

Use only official WhatsApp Business API templates with buyer consent, and follow the Digital Personal Data Protection Act when storing buyer phone numbers and addresses.
