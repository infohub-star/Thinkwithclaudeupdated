Why do Indian taxpayers wait months for income tax refunds, with no clear reason why?

Lakhs of salaried Indians file on time and then wait months for their refund, often stuck on a mismatch or notice they do not understand. Here's "RefundRadar", a refund status explainer a small team could build with Claude in a weekend.

Written by Shantanu Anand, AI Trainer & Consultant · 28 November 2026

Personal Finance & TaxItch Score 74Build time: a weekend

The problem

Who feels it: Salaried employees, pensioners, freelancers and small business owners who overpaid tax through TDS or advance tax. They file their return, see "processing" for months, and then get an automated email or notice about a mismatch they cannot decode, while the money stays locked.

  • Where this came from: Repeated national news coverage in 2025-26 on pending refunds (Business Today, Outlook Money, Business Standard) and taxpayer discussions on Indian personal finance forums.
  • The backlog is large. Reports said over 63 lakh returns for AY 2025-26 were still pending processing (Angel One), and one report described the processing backlog reaching 1 crore returns as the tax department flagged high-value claims (Open).
  • Parliament has noticed. A finance panel raised concern over 27 lakh pending refund cases (CAclubindia).
  • Automated mismatch emails are a common trigger. Refunds have been held after automated emails flagged differences between claims and department data (Outlook Money).
  • Waiting has a deadline cost. Experts warned that delays running past 31 December could block taxpayers from revising returns (Business Standard).

Who's already working on it

Filing platforms such as ClearTax and TaxBuddy sell notice-management plans, usually backed by chartered accountants. An ICAI AI use-case listing describes a notice reply assistant (ICAI AI). The department's own e-Nivaran grievance channel exists but is slow to use. We did not find a low-cost tool focused on explaining why a specific refund is stuck and what the taxpayer should do next.

The solution idea

"RefundRadar" reads your return, Form 26AS/AIS and any department email, tells you in plain language why your refund is stuck, and drafts the exact response or grievance to unblock it.

Mismatch finderUpload your ITR acknowledgement, Form 26AS and AIS; it compares income, TDS and deduction figures and highlights each difference with the amount involved.
Email and notice explainerPaste the department's email or notice; it explains what is being asked, the deadline, and whether a response, revised return or nothing is needed.
Response drafterDrafts the online response, a revised-return checklist, or an e-Nivaran grievance with the right facts filled in.
Status trackerReminds you to check status weekly and alerts you before deadlines such as the 31 December revision cutoff.
CA handoffPackages the case summary for a chartered accountant when the issue is complex.

Weekend build plan

  1. Saturday morning: gather real cases: Collect five masked refund cases from friends or a CA, including their 26AS, AIS and any emails.
  2. Saturday midday: prototype: Ask Claude for an artifact of the refund report and show it to three taxpayers.
  3. Saturday afternoon: extraction: Use Claude Code to build PDF upload and Claude API extraction into structured figures.
  4. Saturday evening: mismatch logic: Write comparison rules and have Claude explain each mismatch in plain language.
  5. Sunday morning: notice explainer: Add the email and notice explainer with the refund-reasons skill.
  6. Sunday midday: drafts: Generate response and grievance drafts, with a clear "review before submitting" step.
  7. Sunday afternoon: reminders: Add weekly reminders and the deadline alert.
  8. Sunday evening: test: Run all five real cases and ask a chartered accountant to check the explanations.

Starter prompt for Claude Code

Build a Next.js + TypeScript + Postgres web app called "RefundRadar" for Indian taxpayers whose income tax refund is delayed.

Flow: user uploads ITR acknowledgement, Form 26AS and AIS PDFs, and optionally pastes a tax department email or notice -> Claude API extracts income, TDS, deductions and refund amounts as JSON -> comparison logic flags mismatches with amounts -> Claude explains in plain English (and Hindi on request) why the refund is likely held and what to do next -> generate a draft response, revised-return checklist or e-Nivaran grievance.

Include: masked-PAN storage, deletion of uploads after 30 days, a weekly reminder email, a 31 December revision-deadline alert, and a clear disclaimer that drafts must be reviewed before submission. Write tests using five sample cases in /fixtures.

Riskiest assumption, and how to test it

Assumption: Many refunds may be held for internal department reasons that no document comparison can reveal, so the tool might often say "nothing to fix, keep waiting" and people may not pay for that.

Test first: Before building, take ten real delayed-refund cases, run their documents and emails through Claude by hand with the refund-reasons skill, and count how many get a concrete action that a chartered accountant agrees with. A reasonable bar: at least six of ten.

Handle PAN and financial documents securely, never file on the user's behalf without explicit consent, position the tool as guidance rather than tax advice, and link every rule to official income tax department guidance.

My take

Most refund anxiety is not the wait itself; it is not knowing whether the wait is normal or whether you are supposed to do something. A tool that answers that one question is worth a lot in tax season.
