Why can't businesses verify new suppliers before purchasing?

Small businesses pay advances to suppliers they found on a marketplace or WhatsApp, and only later learn the GST number was fake or the firm had stopped filing. Here's "VendorCheck", a supplier check report a small team could build with Claude in a weekend.

Written by Shantanu Anand, AI Trainer & Consultant · 19 November 2026

B2B ServicesItch Score 76Build time: a weekend

The problem

Who feels it: Small manufacturers, traders, restaurants and retailers that buy from new suppliers found online, through brokers or on WhatsApp. They usually pay an advance before goods arrive. Large companies have procurement teams and paid verification tools; a small business owner has a GSTIN, a phone number and a feeling.

  • Fake GST registrations are common. A CBIC member said the second nationwide drive against fake registrations found about 10,700 bogus registrations and Rs 10,179 crore of tax evasion as of 22 September 2024. The first drive in 2023 found 21,791 non-existent entities (Outlook Business).
  • The problem continues. The Minister of State for Finance told the Lok Sabha that 3,977 fraudulent GST registrations using forged PAN and Aadhaar were found in FY 2024-25, with estimated evasion of Rs 13,109 crore, and 489 more in FY 2025-26 up to October (Free Press Journal / PTI).
  • A bad supplier costs the buyer too. Vendor compliance gaps under GST are described as the most common reason businesses lose input tax credit (Binary Semantics).
  • The public data exists but is scattered. GSTIN status, return filing and company registration can each be checked, but separately, and guides still walk owners through manual portal lookups (Lendingkart, ClearTax).

Who's already working on it

Verification APIs exist for developers and enterprises. Masters India offers a vendor verification API covering GSTIN, company registration status, address and return filing (Masters India); HyperVerge, Signzy and AuthBridge sell vendor validation and know-your-business checks (HyperVerge, AuthBridge). We didn't find a simple, pay-per-check report built for a small business owner deciding whether to send an advance today.

The solution idea

"VendorCheck" turns a supplier's GSTIN, name and quote into a one-page risk report in two minutes, with a clear "safe to pay advance?" answer and the reasons behind it.

One-input checkEnter a GSTIN or upload the supplier's quote or invoice; it pulls GST status, registration date, filing history and company records through a verification API.
Red-flag scanFlags a cancelled or very new GSTIN, missed returns, a name or address mismatch with the quote, and bank details that don't match the business name.
Plain-language verdictA green, amber or red rating with three reasons in simple English or Hindi, and what to ask the supplier next.
Safer payment termsSuggests a smaller first order, part payment on delivery or escrow when the risk is amber, with a ready message to send the supplier.
Supplier watchlistRe-checks approved suppliers monthly and alerts the owner if a GSTIN is cancelled or filings stop.

Starter prompt for Claude Code

Build a Next.js + TypeScript + Postgres web app called "VendorCheck" for Indian small businesses.

Flow: user enters a supplier GSTIN or uploads a quote/invoice PDF -> Claude API extracts GSTIN, legal name, address, bank account name and IFSC as JSON
-> app calls a GST verification API (behind an interface in /lib/verify.ts so the provider can be swapped) for status, registration date, filing history and legal name
-> deterministic rules in /lib/rules.ts produce flags and a green/amber/red rating
-> Claude writes a 3-reason plain-language verdict and a message to the supplier (English or Hindi).

Rules:
- Red-flag rules live in /rules/supplier-risk.md and /lib/rules.ts with unit tests. The model never decides the rating; it only explains it.
- Every flag must show the data source and the date checked. If data is missing, say "not available"; never guess.
- Store GSTIN checks with timestamps so a monthly re-check can detect changes.
- Show "Based on public records on <date>. Not a guarantee." on every report.

Start by proposing the schema and file structure, then build the GSTIN lookup and rules with tests first.

Riskiest assumption, and how to test it

Assumption: Owners may trust a referral or a phone call more than a report, and may not pay for a check before a small order.

Test first: Run 30 checks by hand for owners in one trade association using Claude and a verification API. Count how many change a payment decision because of the report and how many ask for a second check. A reasonable bar: at least 5 changed decisions and 10 repeat requests.

Use only licensed APIs and public records, follow data protection rules for stored details, and present ratings as information, not an accusation against any supplier.

My take

Owners I train rarely lack the data to check a supplier; they lack the ten minutes and the know-how to read it. The useful part of this build is not the lookup but the verdict: Claude turning five government records into one sentence an owner can act on before sending money.
