Why can't Indian job seekers tell a real job offer from a scam?

Almost every Indian job seeker now sees fake offers, and half can't tell a real recruiter from a scammer. Here's "OfferCheck", a job-offer verifier one person could build with Claude in a weekend.

Written by Shantanu Anand, AI Trainer & Consultant · 11 November 2026

HR & RecruitmentItch Score 78Build time: a weekend

The problem

Who feels it: Freshers, Gen Z candidates and people switching jobs after layoffs, who get offers by WhatsApp, Telegram and email that ask for fees, bank details or ID documents. Genuine employers and HR teams also lose good candidates who now ignore real outreach out of fear.

  • Where this came from: Recent Indian surveys by Indeed and LinkedIn, police and cyber-safety advisories covered by The Week and Free Press Journal, and many recurring job-seeker threads on Indian career subreddits such as r/developersIndia describing fee-based fake offers.
  • Exposure is near-universal. 93% of Indian workers surveyed by Indeed (1,161 respondents) have met suspicious or fraudulent job opportunities, and 51% are not confident they can tell a genuine recruiter from a scammer (CXOToday).
  • Fear also blocks real hiring. At least 75% sometimes or often ignore opportunities they fear are fake, and 14% say they missed genuine opportunities because of that caution (Business Today).
  • Scammers follow one pattern. LinkedIn says 90% of reported scam attempts try to move the chat to personal messaging apps, often in the first message, and 82% of Indian professionals now check for fraud before applying (People Matters).
  • The stakes go beyond lost fees. A Patiala police case cited in an Asia Pacific money-laundering report found fake remote-job recruiters collecting bank accounts and SIM cards, with about Rs 2.99 crore moved through 18 mule accounts (The Week).

Who's already working on it

Job platforms protect their own pages: LinkedIn has added recruiter, company and job-listing verification and ID checks for higher-risk posters (People Matters). The government's CyberDost advisories and the 1930 helpline cover awareness and reporting (The Week). The gap is the offer that arrives off-platform, on WhatsApp or personal email, where nobody checks it before the candidate pays or shares documents.

The solution idea

"OfferCheck" lets a candidate forward any job offer, message or PDF and get a clear verdict in a minute: likely genuine, needs checking, or likely scam, with the reasons and the safe next step.

Forward and checkPaste text, forward a WhatsApp message or upload an offer letter PDF or screenshot.
Red-flag analysisFlags fees, deposits, requests for bank details or OTPs, moves to personal chat apps, and salary far above the market.
Company and domain checkCompares the sender's email domain and website with the company's official domain, and checks domain age.
Safe reply draftsWrites a polite verification email to the company's official HR address and a report text for the cyber crime portal.
Employer verify pageLets genuine companies list their real recruiter emails and domains, so candidates can confirm outreach in one click.

Weekend build plan

  1. Saturday morning — evidence: Collect 30 real job messages (genuine and fake) from friends and placement cells, anonymised. Label each one.
  2. Saturday midday — rules: Write the red-flag checklist as a Claude skill and test it against the 30 labelled messages.
  3. Saturday afternoon — prototype: Ask Claude for an artifact of the paste-and-check screen and show it to five job seekers.
  4. Saturday evening — build: Use Claude Code to build the Next.js app with text, PDF and screenshot upload.
  5. Sunday morning — domain checks: Add email-domain versus official-domain matching and domain-age lookup.
  6. Sunday midday — replies: Add the verification email and cyber-crime report drafts.
  7. Sunday afternoon — WhatsApp: Add a WhatsApp number where candidates forward a message and get the verdict back.
  8. Sunday evening — launch: Share with two college placement cells and one job-seeker community, and measure accuracy on new messages.

Starter prompt for Claude Code

Build a mobile-first Next.js + TypeScript web app called "OfferCheck" for Indian job seekers.

Flow: the user pastes a job message, or uploads a PDF or screenshot -> the server extracts text (OCR for images) -> call the Claude API to extract company name, sender email/phone, role, salary, and any requests (fees, deposits, bank details, Aadhaar, OTP, moving to WhatsApp/Telegram) as JSON -> run checks: sender email domain vs the company's official website domain, domain age via WHOIS, salary vs a simple role-band table -> combine into a verdict (Likely genuine / Needs checking / Likely scam) with a list of reasons -> show the verdict, the reasons in plain English, and two drafted texts: a verification email to official HR and a cyber-crime report.

Include: a red-flag rules file I can edit, 30 sample messages as test fixtures with expected verdicts, a test that reports accuracy, no storage of uploads beyond 24 hours, and a clear note that the result is guidance, not a guarantee.

Riskiest assumption, and how to test it

Assumption: Candidates may not bother checking an offer that looks exciting, which is exactly when they are most vulnerable.

Test first: Give a free WhatsApp check number to two college placement cells for two weeks and count how many students forward an offer without being reminded. A reasonable bar: 100 checks from about 500 students. If use is low, pivot to selling the check to placement cells and HR teams, who will run it for candidates.

Do not call a named company a scam publicly; show reasons and confidence instead. Uploaded offers contain personal data, so get consent, keep them briefly and follow India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act.
