Why do housing societies in India struggle to recover unpaid maintenance dues?

Volunteer committees sit on lakhs in unpaid maintenance because legal recovery is slow and costly. Here's "DuesDesk", a dues-recovery copilot one person could build with Claude in a weekend.

Written by Shantanu Anand, AI Trainer & Consultant · 12 November 2026

Real Estate & HousingItch Score 74Build time: a weekend

The problem

Who feels it: Managing committees of cooperative housing societies and resident welfare associations, usually unpaid volunteers with day jobs. Paying residents feel it too: when a few flats default for months, repairs, security and sinking funds are squeezed for everyone.

  • Where this came from: Recent Indian news reports on society dues disputes in Gurugram, Pune and Mumbai (Business Today, Punekar News, Free Press Journal) and recurring resident complaints about opaque society accounts (Outlook Money).
  • Defaults are common. An estimated 8 in 10 cooperative societies in Pune have members who have not paid for over 12 months, with about Rs 250 crore pending across Pune and PCMC audit records (Punekar News).
  • Few cases ever reach recovery. Of more than 15,000 new defaulter cases a year in Pune, under 20% reach the Deputy Registrar and barely 10% of those end in recovery, because societies pay legal costs upfront and volunteers lack time (Punekar News).
  • Pressure works, but it is crude. A Gurugram township RWA facing Rs 45 lakh in dues put defaulters' names on a board at the gate and recovered Rs 15 lakh in about a month, which sparked public debate (Business Today).
  • Formal orders do come through. A Goregaon society won a Rs 25.40 lakh recovery order against nine defaulters (Free Press Journal).

Who's already working on it

Society apps such as MyGate, NoBrokerHood and ADDA handle billing, online payment and reminders, and ADDA claims 25,000+ communities (Codingclave comparison). Pune's Finio offers legal recovery on a no-recovery, no-fee basis (Punekar News). We did not find a tool that takes a committee from a ledger to a ready-to-file recovery case without a lawyer doing the paperwork.

The solution idea

"DuesDesk" reads a society's maintenance ledger, ranks defaulters, sends polite escalating reminders, and prepares the notices and recovery application the committee needs, so volunteers act in an evening instead of a year.

Ledger importUpload an Excel or app export of bills and payments; it builds each flat's dues, interest per bye-laws and months overdue.
Defaulter rankingSorts flats by amount and age so the committee focuses on the few cases that matter most.
Escalation ladderSends a friendly reminder, a formal reminder and a final notice on a schedule, by email and WhatsApp, with a log of every message.
Recovery packDrafts the statutory notice and the recovery application with a dues statement and evidence list, ready for review and filing.
Member statementGives every member a clear, itemised statement, which also answers the transparency complaints that cause many disputes.

Weekend build plan

  1. Saturday morning — evidence: Get anonymised ledgers from three societies and list how each committee chases dues today.
  2. Saturday midday — rules: Write the interest and notice rules for Maharashtra as a Claude skill and test them on the sample ledgers.
  3. Saturday afternoon — prototype: Ask Claude for an artifact of the defaulter dashboard and show it to two committee treasurers.
  4. Saturday evening — import: Build the Excel import and dues calculation with Claude Code, checked against the treasurers' own numbers.
  5. Sunday morning — reminders: Add the three-step reminder ladder by email, with a message log per flat.
  6. Sunday midday — recovery pack: Generate the notice and recovery application as PDFs, with a clear "review before filing" step.
  7. Sunday afternoon — statements: Add itemised member statements and a shareable summary for the general body meeting.
  8. Sunday evening — pilot: Deploy and onboard one society for a free 30-day pilot.

Starter prompt for Claude Code

Build a Next.js + TypeScript web app called "DuesDesk" for Indian housing society committees.

Flow: the treasurer uploads an Excel/CSV of maintenance bills and payments -> parse and normalise flats, bill dates, amounts and receipts -> call the Claude API to map messy columns and flag anomalies as JSON -> compute each flat's outstanding dues, months overdue and interest using a configurable bye-law rate -> show a ranked defaulter dashboard -> for each defaulter, schedule a 3-step reminder ladder (friendly, formal, final notice) by email, logging every message -> generate a dues statement and a draft recovery notice as PDF for committee review.

Requirements: role-based login for committee members; member-facing itemised statement page; all drafts marked "review before sending"; store data in Postgres; write unit tests for the dues and interest calculation; seed with a 40-flat sample ledger.

Monetisation

Suggested pricing, not yet validated.

  • Free for societies under 50 flats: dashboard and statements.
  • Rs 5 per flat a month for reminders and member statements.
  • Rs 2,999 per recovery pack, or a small share of amounts recovered, through a partner lawyer.
  • Pricing is a suggestion and has not been validated.

Riskiest assumption, and how to test it

Assumption: Committees may not want to escalate against neighbours, even with a ready-made pack, because the social cost feels higher than the money.

Test first: Offer five societies a free ledger clean-up and defaulter report, then count how many send at least one formal reminder within 30 days. A reasonable bar: three of five. If they stall, sell the polite reminder and statement features first and keep recovery as an add-on.

Rules on interest, notices and recovery differ by state and by bye-laws, so every legal draft must be reviewed by the committee or a lawyer. Do not publicly name defaulters, and handle member data under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act.

My take

Society committees are volunteers, and chasing neighbours for money is the job nobody wants. What makes this worth building is that Claude can keep every reminder polite and consistent, so the committee is following a process rather than picking a fight. Start with reminders and a clean ledger; legal notices can come later.
