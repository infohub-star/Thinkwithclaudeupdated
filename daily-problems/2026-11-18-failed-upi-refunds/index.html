Why do Indians wait days to get their money back when a UPI payment fails but the account is debited?

A failed UPI payment that still debits the account is supposed to be reversed quickly, with daily compensation if the bank is late, yet most people never claim it. Here's "RefundTrack", a failed-payment tracker a small team could build with Claude in a weekend.

Written by Shantanu Anand, AI Trainer & Consultant · 18 November 2026

Fintech & Consumer BankingItch Score 77Build time: a weekend

The problem

Who feels it: Anyone who pays by UPI: shoppers, gig workers, students and small shopkeepers. The money leaves the account, the payment shows as failed or pending, and the customer is left chasing the app, the bank and the merchant. Few people know the reversal deadlines or that the bank owes them compensation for each day of delay.

  • Where this came from: Consumer guides from payment apps and banks, personal-finance news, and public complaint threads about failed UPI payments that still debited money (Paytm, Outlook Money, Tech2).
  • There are firm deadlines. For a failed person-to-person UPI transfer, the reversal is due by the next day (T+1); for a failed merchant payment, the bank has up to five days (T+5) (NPCI circular-and-customer-compensation-for-failed-transaction.pdf), Tech2).
  • Late banks owe money. Customers are entitled to Rs 100 for each day the reversal is late beyond those deadlines, for technical failures (Tech2).
  • Digital payments drive complaints. In 2021-22, 42.12% of the 4.18 lakh complaints under the RBI ombudsman schemes were about digital payment modes, the largest category (Orissa Post).
  • Complaint volumes keep rising. The RBI ombudsman received 13.34 lakh complaints in 2024-25, up 13.55% from the year before (Tribune).

Who's already working on it

Payment apps and banks run their own dispute flows inside the app, and publish guides on how to raise a complaint (Paytm). If the bank does not resolve a complaint within 30 days, customers can escalate to the RBI Integrated Ombudsman (Tech2). What is missing is one neutral tool that tracks every failed payment, counts the days owed and drafts each escalation step.

The solution idea

"RefundTrack" reads a screenshot or bank SMS of a failed UPI payment, tells the user the exact reversal deadline and compensation owed, and drafts the complaint and escalation messages at each step.

Screenshot and SMS readerUpload a payment screenshot or paste a bank SMS; it pulls out amount, date, UPI reference number, bank and whether it was a person or merchant payment.
Deadline and compensation calculatorShows the reversal due date and a running count of compensation owed at Rs 100 per late day.
Complaint drafterWrites the in-app dispute text, a bank grievance email and, after 30 days, an ombudsman complaint summary.
Reminder timelineReminds the user when each deadline passes and what to do next.
Case fileKeeps every screenshot, reference number and reply in one place to attach to escalations.

Weekend build plan

  1. Saturday morning: collect examples: Gather 20 redacted failed-payment screenshots and bank SMS messages from friends and colleagues across different apps and banks.
  2. Saturday morning: extraction: Use Claude Code to build an upload screen and a Claude API call that returns amount, date, reference number, bank and payment type as JSON.
  3. Saturday afternoon: rules engine: Encode the T+1 and T+5 deadlines and the Rs 100 per day compensation in a config file, with tests.
  4. Saturday afternoon: drafts: Generate the in-app dispute text, a bank email and an ombudsman summary from each case.
  5. Sunday morning: timeline and reminders: Add a case timeline and scheduled reminders when each deadline passes.
  6. Sunday morning: case summary: Produce a one-page case summary the user can download or share.
  7. Sunday afternoon: test with users: Run 10 real cases with friends, measure how many get reversed or compensated, and fix the drafts.
  8. Sunday evening: launch: Deploy, add a simple landing page and share it in two consumer and fintech communities.

Starter prompt for Claude Code

Build a Next.js + TypeScript + Postgres web app called "RefundTrack" that helps Indian users recover money from failed UPI payments.

Flow: user uploads a payment screenshot or pastes a bank SMS -> Claude API extracts amount, date, UPI reference number, bank, app and payment type (person or merchant) as JSON -> a rules engine computes the reversal due date (T+1 for person-to-person, T+5 for merchant) and compensation owed (Rs 100 per late day), with rules in a config file -> show a case page with the deadline, a running compensation counter and next step -> generate three drafts: in-app dispute text, bank grievance email, and an RBI ombudsman complaint summary after 30 days -> daily scheduled job sends reminders when deadlines pass.

Add email login, a case list, file storage for screenshots, unit tests for the rules engine, and a short disclaimer that the app gives information, not legal advice. Start by proposing the data model and folder structure, then build step by step.

Riskiest assumption, and how to test it

Assumption: Most failed payments may reverse on their own within a day, so users may not feel enough pain to install a tool.

Test first: Before building, post a simple form in two consumer communities asking people to share their last failed UPI payment that was not reversed on time. Handle 15 cases by hand with Claude and track how many are still unresolved after the deadline and how many people come back for a second case. If at least a third are still open after the deadline, build.

The app must give information, not legal advice, and must never ask for UPI PINs, passwords or one-time passwords. Screenshots and bank SMS are personal financial data, so store them securely and follow India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act.
